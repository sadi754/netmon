Connection

NetMon reads the router itself, in your local network, once a minute while it runs (RouterOS 7.1 or newer, over its REST API). The router keeps counting while this PC is off, so that traffic still arrives – shared out evenly over those hours and marked as an estimate. If the router restarts while NetMon isn't running, the traffic since NetMon's last reading is lost. For a record that never misses anything, use a relay.

Paste the script into the router's terminal (Winbox → New Terminal), then press Test. It makes a read-only user “netmon” with a random password – NetMon keeps it encrypted for your Windows user – and, if it's off, turns on the router's “www” service for your local network only.

NetMon reads your router's traffic from a NetMon relay: a small program on a web host (PHP, or Cloudflare) that your MikroTik reports to every minute – so nothing is missed while this PC is off. See relay/README.md to host your own.

About

NetMon – a local network monitor for Windows. Everything runs on this PC; no accounts. Free and open source (MIT): github.com/sadi754/netmon

  • IP geolocation by DB-IP (CC BY 4.0), downloaded on request
  • Packet capture: Npcap (installed separately)
  • Country borders: Natural Earth (public domain)
  • globe.gl with three.js, uPlot, country-flag-icons (MIT)
  • Python: Starlette, uvicorn, websockets, pywebview, pystray, Pillow, maxminddb
